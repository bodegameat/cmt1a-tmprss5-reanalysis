White Paper

Abstract & Implications

Charcot–Marie–Tooth disease type 1A (CMT1A) is driven by PMP22 gene dosage, yet circulating biomarkers that report Schwann-cell pharmacodynamics remain incompletely validated across tissue compartments. Wang et al. (2020) reported plasma TMPRSS5 (spinesin) elevation in CMT1A with a 2.07-fold increase and discriminative performance of AUC 0.9131, nominating TMPRSS5 as a candidate blood protein marker. Reanalysis of the Zhao/Svaren antisense oligonucleotide (ASO) sciatic-nerve RNA-seq cohort (GSE103799) demonstrates that Tmprss5 transcript abundance tracks disease and treatment in peripheral nerve: untreated C22 mice exhibit a +2.90 log2 fold-change versus wild-type, ASO-mediated Pmp22 suppression yields a −1.26 log2 fold-change relative to untreated C22, and the disease-associated TPM excess is reduced by 67.40%. In contrast, the Svaren 2019 Dryad cutaneous (skin) microarray resource shows a cutaneous null for TMPRSS5 (fold-change 1.00, p = 0.99, ranking #52 of 54 probes), indicating that skin transcript abundance does not recapitulate the nerve or plasma signal.

The 67.4% reduction in sciatic-nerve Tmprss5 after Pmp22-directed ASO treatment places TMPRSS5 downstream of Schwann-cell gene-dosage pathology and within the dynamic range of a treatment-responsive transcript. The cutaneous null argues against skin biopsy mRNA as a surrogate for this axis. Plasma protein elevation (Wang 2020) remains the most clinically accessible compartment, yet the missing link is whether circulating TMPRSS5 quantitatively tracks nerve electrophysiology and ASO engagement in the same individuals over time. Future studies should therefore prioritize paired plasma TMPRSS5 protein assays with nerve conduction / electromyography endpoints to establish whether blood levels report the same pharmacodynamic window observed at +2.90 / −1.26 log2FC in GSE103799.
